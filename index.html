Why Here

Denver International Airport isn't a stop on the route. It's the route.

DIA moves more passengers than almost any airport in the country, and nearly all of them still need a ride once they land. Aurora CyberHub is positioning fleet and charging infrastructure directly in that corridor — ahead of Cybercab availability in Colorado — so the company is operational the day the vehicles arrive, not scrambling to catch up.

1
High-traffic corridor: Tower Road / Green Valley Ranch / DIA, positioned for airport-driven ride demand
2
Charging-first model: on-site DCFC infrastructure serves the fleet and generates independent revenue
3
Early-mover position: infrastructure and site work underway ahead of Colorado Cybercab deployment
Service Area

The DIA corridor, mapped for fleet operations.

Site selection is focused on Aurora's Airport District, I-1 and I-2 zoned parcels — chosen for direct highway access to DIA and clearance from the 300-ft residential buffer.

How It Will Work

From landing to destination, one dispatch.

STEP 01

Request

Riders request a cab from the app the moment they land — no shuttle line, no rideshare surge guesswork.

STEP 02

Ride

An autonomous EV picks up curbside and routes directly to Aurora, Green Valley Ranch, or greater Denver.

STEP 03

Recharge

Between rides, the fleet returns to the Aurora CyberHub charging pad — keeping vehicles local and turnaround fast.
